Every time a golden, crispy French fry shatters between your teeth, have you ever wondered about the science behind its deliciousness? In the global fast-food industry, **McCain potatoes (Russet Burbank)** have long been the “golden ingredient” for making the perfect French fry. This article unveils the unique charm of this legendary potato and explains why it continues to reign supreme in the world of fries.

## I. The “Innate Superiority” of McCain Potatoes
### 1. The Perfect Starch Ratio
McCain potatoes boast a **dry matter content of 20-22%**, with **amylose making up approximately 21%**. This unique molecular structure forms an even pore network during frying, creating the signature crispy exterior and fluffy interior. Compared to ordinary potato varieties with **15-18% dry matter content**, McCain fries are **up to 30% crispier**.

### 2. Low Sugar & Anti-Browning Properties
With a naturally **low sucrose content of just 0.2%**, McCain potatoes are less prone to excessive Maillard reactions during frying. Studies show that fries made from regular potatoes brown **2.3 times faster** than McCain fries—this is the key reason why fast-food chains can maintain that iconic golden color.

### 3. Ideal Shape & Thick Flesh
With an **average length of 15 cm** and a **diameter of 6 cm**, McCain potatoes are perfectly structured for fry production. Their **flesh thickness of 1.2-1.5 cm** ensures a **78% fry yield**, far surpassing the **62% yield of round potato varieties**.
